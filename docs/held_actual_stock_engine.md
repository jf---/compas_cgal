# Held Actual-Stock Engine (design)

**Today, replaying the actual stock is slow because of two constant costs, and stock size is only a third.** Every sweep rebuilds a point locator over the whole arrangement, which is O(n log n). On top of that, a sweep on even a tiny arrangement costs milliseconds of CPU, and an exact station a large fraction of a millisecond (section 2, generated). Windowing the stock removes the size term and leaves both constants.

The design is one persistent exact arrangement per generation:

- Every face carries the index of the last sweep that covered it.
- A single zone walk then reads both views a circle needs: the exact actual stock and the sound last-16 bound.
- The locator is attached once and updated incrementally.
- Edges that no future view separates are garbage-collected, so the arrangement holds only the stock boundary plus the last 16 sweeps.
- Station classification gets an interval filter, with exact one-root evaluation as the fallback.

Budget: Monstera's full replay plus one decision per circle in ≤ 5 s on one core. That allows 0.25 ms per sweep and 0.6 ms per query (13 µs per station). Today's engine is far over both; the measured state is the first generated table in section 2, and the red test `benchmarks/acceptance/test_actual_stock_engine_budget_slow.py` holds the line.

**Option (c) cannot be had by skipping circles.** On the first 300 circles of Figure 5, the rule "advance along the emitted ladder until the actual stock reaches the cap" dropped none of them once coverage was required. Coverage stopped 227 advances; the cap stopped 71. Every rung removes material that its neighbours do not. Placement on the actual stock therefore has to move circles (centre and radius) along the guide. Removing rungs is not enough.

Status: DESIGN with a reproducible cost profile (stage S0). No native code exists yet. Section 2's tables are generated by `tools/actual_stock_profile.py`, and each states the load it was measured under. Process CPU time is used throughout. The probe that preceded the tool ran at about ten times the load of the recorded run and produced CPU values within a few percent of it, so the earlier guess that loaded CPU numbers were inflated about 2.2× does not hold for these single-threaded native calls.

Readings:

- **Subtraction grows linearly with the arrangement** (sequential table, circle and connector columns). `subtract_region_local` builds a private `Arr_trapezoid_ric_point_location` over the whole arrangement on every sweep (`src/stock_local_2.cpp:338`). The surgery itself is local.
- **The first query after any mutation pays a second rebuild.** `Stock2::set()` resets the cached locator. That rebuild is the cold − warm column, which grows with the arrangement like the subtraction does.
- **A warm query does not grow with size.** Its per-station cost stays in the same band from the smallest to the largest arrangement. That is exact classification: one-root `Sqrt_extension` over lazy `Epeck::FT`, and about half of it is allocation according to the spatial audit.
- **The budget runs die in the first hundred-odd sweeps.** Even while the arrangement is still small, the per-sweep and per-query means are tens of times over the Monstera split.
- **A window removes the size term and nothing else.** The windowed table leaves a per-sweep constant of milliseconds even at a few dozen halfedges, and the same per-station cost. A window cannot meet the budget either, because the exact window needs hundreds of sweeps per query: heavy overlap of Held circles makes every window deep.
- **The last-16 bound is not tight in the tail** (gap line): the median gap is zero, but the p90 and maximum are tens of degrees, with 0 bound breaks. Near the cap, many last-16 refusals were overturned by the exact stock (section 6). The fast path therefore cannot be the bound alone.

## 3. Formulation

### 3.1 One labelled arrangement, two views

`StockReplay2` is a new native class, separate from `Stock2`, whose `Gps` stays the reference. It holds an `Arrangement_2<Gps_circle_segment_traits_2<Epeck>, Arr_extended_dcel<…, FaceLabel>>`:

- `FaceLabel` is one of `OUTSIDE` (not in Ω), `UNCUT`, or `CUT(k)`, where k is the largest emission index of a sweep covering the face.
- **Insert sweep k:** insert its exact boundary curves with the persistent locator, which is the same curves and seeds as `LocalRemovalRegion`. Flood the region's faces exactly as `subtract_region_local` does, and set `CUT(k)` on each, since k is now the maximum.
- **Query circle i:** zone-walk each station's tool circle once. A face is material in the **exact view** iff it is `UNCUT`. It is material in the **last-m view** iff it is `UNCUT` or `CUT(k)` with k < i − m. Both runs come out of the same walk, so "exact replay on refusal" costs nothing extra.
- **Garbage collection after query i:** remove an edge between labels a and b when no future view separates them. That holds when both are `CUT` with max(a, b) < i − m, or when a = b. The merged face keeps max(a, b). The arrangement then stays at stock boundary plus last-16 boundaries, about the size of today's Gps plus ~16 × 30 edges near the front.
- **Persistent locator:** attach `Arr_trapezoid_ric_point_location` once, as an arrangement observer, and never reset it on mutation. Landmarks is the measured alternative behind the same interface (stage S2). Emission order is spatially coherent, which is the wrong case for RIC's random-order expectation, so both are benchmarked. A clone gets its own re-attached locator and is never shared (Copying a `Gps`, [Exact-Kernel Discipline](exactness.md)).

A `Gps` cannot be used here: its representation invariant (`representation_is_valid`) forbids edges between faces of equal containment, and the labels need exactly those edges.

### 3.2 Local exact stock and spatial search

- **Generation needs no window gathering.** The locator plus the zone walk already touch only the query's neighbourhood. `box_intersection_d` (outward-rounded `to_interval` boxes of the sweep extents) is kept for the two batch jobs:
  - the coverage-of-dropped-circle checks;
  - an independent, parallel re-check of a single circle's last-16 certificate from the emitted path.
- **`spatial_sort` does not apply to the generator.** Each query must see exactly the sweeps before it, so insertion is in emission order. A batch of sweeps between two queries commutes as a set difference, so its order changes only cost. `spatial_sort` helps only bulk window builds (the batch jobs above).
- **AABB / `Interval_skip_list`:** not needed in the engine. The zone walk is the arc-stabbing query.

### 3.3 Station cost: interval filter, exact fallback

`classify_audit_unguarded_station_exact` gets a filtered front end:

1. Evaluate each tool-circle × curve crossing, its angular order on the rim, and the chord-squared run test against the exact cap surrogate in `Interval_nt`. The inputs are exact injected doubles and `to_interval` of each `Sqrt_extension`.
2. Decide only when every sign is certified non-zero. Otherwise re-run that station with today's exact one-root code.

This is CGAL's `Filtered_predicate` pattern. A filter failure costs one exact station, never a wrong answer. `sign_mixed_radical` already shows the idiom (`docs/number_types.md` rule R1).

### 3.4 Exactness and soundness

- **Number types.** All geometry is circle-segment traits over Epeck: one-root numbers, with no CORE and no `Epeck_with_sqrt`. Labels are integers. GC compares integers only. Every geometric decision is a traits predicate. Doubles enter once, at the binding.
- **The replayed stock contains the true stock.** Circles are exact annuli. Non-Pythagorean connector pieces use the certified under-rectangle, so they remove a subset of the true capsule: replayed ⊇ true (`Stock_UNDER`).
- **Engagement only shrinks with stock.** Max-run and total engagement at a station are monotone in the stock. So "within cap" on the exact view is within cap on the true stock. The same holds for the last-m view, which is Ω minus a subset of the sweeps and therefore contains the exact view. A cap breach on `Stock_UNDER` is only a candidate until it is bracketed with `Stock_OVER`. Placement never needs that bracket, because it only accepts within-cap rungs.
- **NOTHING verdicts.** A circle that removes NOTHING from `Stock_UNDER` removes nothing from the true stock (true ⊆ UNDER), so that drop decision is sound.
- **The filter.** An interval verdict is certified, or it falls back to exact. Never an epsilon.
- **Stations.** These are 45 exact stations, a sampling density. Nothing is claimed between stations, the same as the existing gate. The ruled circle certificate is the exact last-16 view; the generator decides on the exact view, which is tighter.

## 4. Prototype measurements

All runs: `build/actual-stock-engine/run.sh` under `pixi run --no-install`, with `SKBUILD_EDITABLE_SKIP` set to the meta-path build directory.

- `tools/actual_stock_profile.py` (the port of `profile_engine.py`): section 2. It confirms the cost model's three terms: per-sweep constant, per-station constant, and the O(n log n) locator rebuild. It also confirms that a window removes only the last of these.
- Last-16 bound vs exact: 0 breaks on the sampled circles (a break raises); the tail gap is in the generated gap line.
- The labelled arrangement cannot be prototyped in Python, because the bindings do not expose face labels. Its cost model is the small-arrangement rows above: each sweep at the last-1 window's per-sweep cost, plus one warm query per circle. The generated projection line in section 2 applies today's constants to Monstera's counts; it lands more than an order of magnitude over budget before the filter and the sweep-constant work (stages S4 and S5).

## 6. Placement (option c): what the decision rule changes

Today's rule: place the next circle where the predecessor model (Ω minus the previous disk) reaches the cap. On the actual stock this is pessimistic. The explore run shows a median of 15.2° actual against 61.9° modelled.

The candidate rule is to advance until the actual-stock engagement reaches the cap. As a ladder, stop at the first exceed, because engagement is not monotone in spacing.

Prototype: `build/actual-stock-engine/placement_thin.py`. This section's numbers are not yet regenerable by a pixi task; porting the probe into `tools/` belongs to stage S6, which replaces it. It applies the rule to the emitted ladder of Figure 5's first 300 circles, decides with the last-16 bound, and re-decides each refusal exactly.

| variant | kept / 300 | circle length | uncovered dropped | stops: cap / coverage | last-16 refusals overturned by exact |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- | ---: |
| coverage required (every skipped circle removes NOTHING after kept + candidate + connectors) | 300 | 4,542 mm, unchanged | 0 | 71 / 227 | 52 of 123 |
| cap only; coverage checked afterwards against the final stock, including every later circle | 98 | 2,847 mm (−37 %) | **202 of 202** | 93 / – | 14 of 107 |

JSONs: `figure5_refined.thin.cov.limit300.h32.json` and `figure5_refined.thin.limit300.h32.json`. CPU time 408 s and 167 + 378 s under load.

Readings:

- **Engagement allows skipping; coverage forbids it.** Out of 299 advances, 227 stopped on coverage and only 71 on the cap. Each Held circle cuts a crescent that no neighbour cuts. Its low actual engagement comes from earlier sweeps having emptied most of its rim, not from redundancy. The cap alone would cut circle length by 37 %, but every dropped circle leaves uncut material, even after all 2,010 circles have run.
- **Under-loading is a geometry problem, not a spacing problem.** Option (c) has to move the centre and radius along the guide: larger steps where the actual stock is thin, then a coverage proof on the moved circle. That is stage S6. Its circle-count and length change cannot be estimated by thinning, and this probe gives no number for it.
- **The bound must not be the only fast path near the cap.** 42 % of last-16 refusals were false (52 of 123; 14 of 107 in the cap-only run). The labelled engine reads the exact view in the same walk, so placement decides on the exact view and records the last-16 view as the certificate.
